# E&O Binder Grounding

Underwriter clearance procedures and 2025-2026 generative-AI insurability guidance, researched from primary sources (E&O applications, broker guidance, law firm alerts) on 2026-08-08.


## The clearance procedures (as underwriters phrase them)


**1. Script Clearance Review (All Stages)**  
The script must be read prior to commencement of production to eliminate matter that is defamatory, invades privacy, or is otherwise actionable, and the shooting script and rough cuts must be re-checked through final cut since dialogue and persons may be added during photography.  
*Fed by:* BRAND, PERSON, MUSIC, ARTWORK, LOCATION, MEDIA, ORGANIZATION, OTHER · [source](https://www.frontrowinsurance.com/news/film-eo-insurance-clearance-procedures-for-producers-part-1-of-3/)


**2. Copyright Report and Chain of Title**  
Unless the work is an unpublished original not based on any other work, a copyright report covering domestic and foreign copyrights and renewal rights must be obtained on the property and any underlying copyrighted material.  
*Fed by:* documents — outside a script scan · [source](https://asset.trvstatic.com/download/assets/ee-eo-03.doc/164e06cc63ca11eeb95a06b700163137)


**3. Title Report**  
Prior to final title selection, a title report must be obtained from a recognized title clearance service, and the title must be changed if the report shows conflicting prior uses.  
*Fed by:* TITLE · [source](https://www.frontrowinsurance.com/errors-omissions-insurance-101)


**4. Origins of Original Work and Similar Submissions**  
If the script is an unpublished original, the origins of its basic idea, sequence of events, and characters must be ascertained, together with whether similar properties have been submitted to the applicant and why any submitting party could not claim theft or infringement.  
*Fed by:* documents — outside a script scan · [source](https://asset.trvstatic.com/download/assets/ee-eo-03.doc/164e06cc63ca11eeb95a06b700163137)


**5. Releases from Recognizable Living Persons**  
No names, faces, or likenesses of recognizable living persons — including thinly disguised versions identifiable from context — may be used without written releases granting the right to edit, juxtapose, and fictionalize, dispensed with only for fleeting background or with written justification accepted by the insurer.  
*Fed by:* PERSON · [source](https://asset.trvstatic.com/download/assets/ee-eo-03.doc/164e06cc63ca11eeb95a06b700163137)


**6. Fictitious Name Checks (Full Cast Clearance)**  
For fictional works, a full cast script clearance check must be performed on all character names, business names, and similar identifiers with all recommended changes made, and even deceased persons must be cleared for publicity rights where there is considerable fictionalization.  
*Fed by:* PERSON, ORGANIZATION, BRAND · [source](https://kellyinsurancegroup.com/film-production-television-producers-errors-omissions-clearance-procedures/)


**7. Primary Sources for Portrayals of Actual Events**  
If the production portrays actual events or persons, the author's sources must be shown to be independent and primary (contemporaneous news reports, court transcripts, witness interviews) rather than secondary copyrighted works such as another author's book or magazine articles.  
*Fed by:* TRUESTORY, PERSON · [source](https://asset.trvstatic.com/download/assets/ee-eo-03.doc/164e06cc63ca11eeb95a06b700163137)


**8. Music Synchronization and Performance Licenses**  
Synchronization and performance licenses must be obtained from the composer or copyright owner of all music used, plus master-use licenses from recording owners for previously recorded music, unless both the composition and its arrangement are in the public domain.  
*Fed by:* MUSIC · [source](https://www.insuredproduction.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/07/UA-Producer%E2%80%99s-EO-Application-EO-Form.pdf)


**9. Film Clip and Stock Footage Licenses**  
For every film clip or stock element, authorization must be obtained from the owner and from all contributors to the clip — underlying literary and musical rights holders, actors, and musicians — with new synchronization and performance licenses secured for any music embedded in the clip.  
*Fed by:* MEDIA · [source](https://www.insuredproduction.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/07/UA-Producer%E2%80%99s-EO-Application-EO-Form.pdf)


**10. Location and Distinctive Property Releases**  
Written releases must be secured for distinctive locations, buildings, businesses, artwork, personal property, or products that are filmed, with releases unnecessary only where real property appears as non-distinctive background.  
*Fed by:* LOCATION, ARTWORK, BRAND · [source](https://asset.trvstatic.com/download/assets/ee-eo-03.doc/164e06cc63ca11eeb95a06b700163137)


**11. Creator Agreements and All-Media Distribution Rights**  
Written agreements must exist with all creators, writers, performers, and providers of material or on-screen services, and all contracts and releases must grant the applicant the right to market the production in all media and markets, with any gaps in underlying rights notified to the insurer.  
*Fed by:* documents — outside a script scan · [source](https://asset.trvstatic.com/download/assets/ee-eo-03.doc/164e06cc63ca11eeb95a06b700163137)


**12. Generative AI Usage Disclosure**  
All generative AI use — script material, imagery, music, voices, likenesses, or vendor AI-assisted work — must be disclosed on the application, specifying which tools were used at which production stages and what percentage of content involved AI, with documented consent for any digital replica; non-disclosure at the application stage is the fastest path to a denied claim.  
*Fed by:* AI · [source](https://www.akkerins.com/new-blog/ai-film-production-insurance-gap-2026)


## Generative-AI insurability guidance (2025-2026)


**AI-generated script or dialogue text**  
*Risk:* The US Copyright Office's January 2025 report holds that purely AI-generated output is not copyrightable and prompts alone are not authorship, so AI-written script elements carry no copyright the producer can warrant — undermining the chain-of-title representations every E&O application requires.  
*Cure:* Document human creative contributions element-by-element (selection, arrangement, substantial rewriting) so each script element is defensibly human-authored, and disclose the AI-assisted workflow on the application.  
[source](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2025/02/copyrightability-of-ai-outputs-us-copyright-office-analyzes-human-authorship-requirement)


**AI-generated voice/narration and voice cloning**  
*Risk:* Voice cloning spans IP, privacy, right-of-publicity, and fraud exposure (voice-actor suits against Lovo and ElevenLabs), and carriers anticipate AI- and biometric-specific sublimits or exclusions; underwriters already ask about written consent from voice sources and biometric data safeguards.  
*Cure:* Obtain written consent from every voice source, use vetted third-party AI vendors under proper contracts rather than in-house tools, and document biometric storage and misuse safeguards — or replace with a human voice performer under a standard agreement.  
[source](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/technology/insurers-sound-the-alarm-on-ai-voice-clonings-growing-liability-risks-559294.aspx)


**Digital replicas, de-aging, and AI likenesses of performers**  
*Risk:* SAG-AFTRA's digital-replica protections have raised the standard underwriters apply — they expect documented consent, scope, and compensation for any AI-generated likeness, and E&O applications have stalled until explicit written cloning consent could be shown; AI likenesses of real actors without consent (the ByteDance deepfake action) are treated as uninsurable exposure.  
*Cure:* Contract for written, per-use consent to each digital replica with defined scope and compensation, audit rights, and destruction or retirement of the replica at contract expiration, and produce that paper to the underwriter before binding.  
[source](https://rodriqueslaw.com/blog/ai-clauses-entertainment-contracts/)


**AI-generated imagery, footage, or finished visual content**  
*Risk:* Many 2025-2026 E&O renewals added endorsements excluding AI-generated content, and several major carriers apply absolute AI exclusions: generative imagery has no clean chain of title because models are trained on unlicensed works, so standard clearance review cannot be completed and non-disclosure is the fastest path to a denied claim.  
*Cure:* Disclose to the broker before applying which tools were used, at which stages, and what percentage of content involved AI; get written confirmation the policy responds to AI-related copyright claims — or replace generative elements with licensed stock or human-created assets.  
[source](https://www.akkerins.com/new-blog/ai-film-production-insurance-gap-2026)


**AI-generated or AI-assisted music and score**  
*Risk:* Media underwriters treat generative AI as an unclearable-rights problem — 'all content is owned by somebody' but traditional rights-clearing from composers and providers is impossible for model output, with the NYT v. OpenAI training-data litigation driving carrier nervousness about untraceable content origins.  
*Cure:* Use a human composer or licensed library music under standard sync/master licenses; where AI assists, track content origins and establish a responsibility chain with the AI tool vendor so provenance can be shown to the underwriter.  
[source](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/breaking-news/generative-ai-presents-eando-risks-for-media-and-entertainment-firms-493253.aspx)


**Undisclosed AI use by post-production vendors (VFX, editorial, composers)**  
*Risk:* Verisk/ISO generative-AI exclusions effective January 2026 (CG 40 47 et al.) strip the fallback GL coverage for personal/advertising injury where likeness and copyright claims traditionally landed, so any vendor-introduced AI content that surfaces post-binding leaves the production with no E&O and no CGL response.  
*Cure:* Extend WGA-style disclosure mandates to all vendor agreements — require disclosure whenever AI tools are used, plus warranties that no AI content was trained on unlicensed data, with indemnity shifting to whichever party introduced the AI input.  
[source](https://www.independentagent.com/vu_resource/verisk-to-roll-out-new-general-liability-exclusions-for-generative-ai-exposures/)
